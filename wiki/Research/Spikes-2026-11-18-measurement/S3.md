## Verdict: REDESIGN (not kill)

- v2 did not mis-call the two high-confidence outcomes in the harmful direction: lft is rejected (via the per-task veto, unreplicated); pth is not rejected.
- But two redesign criteria fired. (a) **Accuracy non-inferiority resolved on 0 of 24 comparisons** (pre-registered redesign trigger: < 15%). The only v2 verdicts that are not `need-more-cells` are 6 `reject-veto-pending`. (b) **v2 gives pth no efficiency credit** (FLAT, not SAVES), so the one new path the plan adds (efficiency win) did not fire on the one real efficiency lever we have.

## Why NI cannot resolve (the main finding)

The CI is the wider of Newcombe on pooled discriminative cells and a task-clustered t-interval. Discriminative tasks per tier are 0 to 7 (median 1-2 in the small datasets), so the t-interval has 0 to 6 df and half-widths of 80 to 800 pp. The "n/cell for NI" column of `s3-tables.md` says what resolution would take: 43 to 297 runs per cell per tier (typical 100-200), against the 3-5 we run. The audit's "accuracy cannot be both cheap and decisive on binary H3" is confirmed on real data, and the pre-registered CI rule makes it worse than necessary where tasks are few. MDE at observed discordance (column MDE pp, Gaussian, not observed-effect power) is 5 to 79 pp, typically 11 to 18 pp.

## Efficiency axis: v2 as pre-registered misses the pth win

pth, per-task log token ratio (cand/base): cogito -1.32, +0.02, +0.04, +0.03, +0.27, +0.02; gemma -1.27, +0.21, +0.13, -0.24, +0.35, 0.00. One task (todo-heavy, 3.5x fewer tokens, 13 turns to 3) carries the whole effect; five of six are flat. The unweighted mean geometric ratio is 0.85 / 0.87 with t-CI [0.47, 1.57] / [0.47, 1.62], so FLAT. Tokens per solved run (candidate/baseline) is 0.61 / 0.67, which does show it. The equal-weight-over-tasks estimator (plan rule 7) answers "does the lever help a typical task", while the true claim is "it removes a cost where calls are independent". This is trigger dilution (see S4) inside the gate itself.

I did not reproduce the audit's figure on real data. The two are not contradictory (different task mixes, halves of 2-4), but the audit number should be quoted as simulated.

## Clustering (tasks as clusters)

- **Single-arm pooled pass rate** (20 full-suite baseline arms, 12 tasks x k=5): clustered SE / naive binomial SE has median **2.07** (range 1.8-2.2). Treating the 60 cells of an arm as independent understates the SE by half. Naive SE about 6 pp, clustered about 13 pp.
- **Arm difference** (task-level paired SE, task means as the unit, Miller 2411.00640 with runs averaged within task) vs naive unpaired SE: ratio < 1 for homogeneous-effect comparisons (v019-rerun 0.50-0.56, calib-v3 0.33-0.35, recent-* 0.40-0.78, stl-h3 grok 0.25), because task pairing removes between-task variance. Ratio > 1 where the effect is heterogeneous: lft gemma 1.47, strat-switch cogito 1.44, 2f qwen 1.85. Several tiers show 0.0 because every task is exactly equal in both arms (zero variance; not evidence of precision).
- Consequence: the gate's pairing by task is the right idea (it pays off, up to 3x tighter SE), but only where the effect is homogeneous, and its between-task term is what blows up when one task moves.

## pass^k vs pass rate (baseline arms)

pass^k = mean over tasks of C(c,k)/C(n,k) (tau-bench), averaged over the tasks with n >= k (timeouts leave uneven n; a few qwen arms are slightly non-monotone for that reason). pass@k is also in the table.

- Median pass^5 / pass^1 over 20 arms: **0.80** (P-S3-5 predicted < 0.8: borderline, not met). By tier: cogito:8b arms lose the most (11-03: 0.28 to 0.17, 11-04 d9: 0.35 to 0.17, 11-18 final: 0.47 to 0.33), qwen3:4b and grok-4 lose little (0.66 to 0.60 on grok-4).
- Reported pass rate hides this: cogito pass rate 28-53% but pass^5 17-42%.

## ICC(1) and task discriminativeness

Form used: one-way ANOVA ICC(1) = (MSB - MSW) / (MSB + (n0 - 1) MSW) with unbalanced n0, on binary solve, tasks as groups. (The simplified sb2/(sb2+sw2) of the coordinator note agrees when balanced.) Median ICC(1) over 20 arms = **0.80**, range 0.58-0.90: "good" by the >= 0.75 threshold. Tasks, not runs, carry the variance. SE(ICC) was not computed; with 12 tasks per arm it will not reach the 0.03 target.

Discriminative tasks, cells pooled across all baseline arms per tier (full lists in `s3-tables.md` section D):

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| qwen3:4b | 12 | 5 | 3 | h3-plan-only, h3-todo-heavy, rw-6 |

ab-trap-4 and h3-continuation are saturated on every tier; rw-2 and rw-7 are floor on every local tier. rw-6 is the one task discriminative on all tiers. These classes are pooled over code versions, so they are descriptive, not stable.

## Redesign recommendations for v2 (for the ablation-warden spec; nothing implemented)

1. Efficiency primary: tokens per solved run and total-token ratio with a within-task run-level permutation/bootstrap, plus a per-task heterogeneity flag (a lever that saves 70% on 1 of 6 tasks is "conditional win", not FLAT).
2. Accuracy NI on 1-3 discriminative tasks must not use t(df=0..2). Use a stratified (fixed-suite) exact or permutation interval, and state `need N` rather than a CI of +/-800 pp. Report the n/cell column from `s3-tables.md` on every receipt.
3. Veto: keep the plan's automatic k=5 solo rerun; a retrospective veto fired on 4 non-harmful comparisons.
4. Keep NI as a long-run, zero-token-tier-fed claim only; accuracy resolution at n <= 5 is not available.
5. Prune the always-pass/always-fail tasks from Tier-1 runs: 5-9 of 12 tasks per tier carry no accuracy information.

## Caveats

- Labels are thin (3 harmful/efficiency with medium+ confidence). The concordance table cannot prove v2 right, only fail it.
- Cell-level v1 uses graded `accuracy`; v2 uses binary `solved`. They differ on partial credit (e.g. cogito required-tools 0.67 accuracy at 0/3 solved).
- Mixed-code arms (e.g. recent-*, v019-*) are not clean A/B pairs.
- 4 comparisons (bat-custom/h3mi2 for S4, fam-*) lack traces; S3 uses only the results JSON so they are included here.
